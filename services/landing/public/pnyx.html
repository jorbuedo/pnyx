Private governance on Midnight

Speak freely. Prove you belong. Stay unlinkable.

Pnyx is a community forum where membership is something you prove, not something you advertise — and a self-custody wallet for Cardano, Midnight and beyond. One app. No accounts, no passwords, no profile following you from room to room.

The whole idea

Privacy that is structural, not a setting

Most apps ask you to trust a promise. Pnyx is built so the promise is hard to break — the things that would identify you are never collected, never published, and in most cases never leave your phone.

Your keys are your account

There is no sign-up, no email, no password to forget or leak. A key on your device is the identity, unlocked by your fingerprint or face, and a signature is how you walk through any door.

A different you in every room

You join each community under a persona that exists only there. The chain records that a member joined, never which identity. Two of your personas cannot be linked to each other, and neither can be traced back to you.

Votes nobody can read

Cast a vote and the result is counted on chain without anyone — moderators and us included — learning how you voted. Double-voting is impossible by construction, not by policy.

Communities

Every community is its own contract

Creating a community deploys a smart contract on Midnight, and its address is derived from what it was created with. So anyone can check that the community in front of them is the one it claims to be — without asking us, and without taking our word for it.

  • Channels and polls in a layout that already feels familiar — community rail, channel list, conversation.
  • Bans that actually hold. Joining spends a one-per-community marker derived from your identity, so a removed member cannot stroll back in under a new persona.
  • Invite-only, or proof-gated. A founder can require an invite code — or set a condition a Cardano wallet can prove, and let the invite be issued on chain, automatically.
  • Direct messages outside every community, end-to-end encrypted with MLS. The relay moves bytes it cannot read.
No hand-waving

What goes on the ledger, and what never does

"On-chain" is not automatically a virtue. A public ledger is permanent and readable by everyone, for ever — which is exactly right for a membership proof and exactly wrong for a conversation. So we split them, deliberately.

On chain, forever, verifiable

  • Membership — as an anonymous leaf
  • Votes — tallied, never attributed
  • Moderation — bans and moderators
  • Your identity anchor — a commitment, not a name

Off chain, on purpose

  • Posts and replies
  • Channels and descriptions
  • Direct messages — E2E encrypted
  • Anything that reads like a sentence

Zero-knowledge proofs are generated on your phone, on-device, by a native Rust prover. The thing that proves you are a member never needs to tell anyone who you are — and the secret it is built from never leaves the handset.

Wallet

A real wallet, not a login button

The identity that gets you into a community is deliberately kept apart from the money. Next to it sits a full self-custody wallet: Cardano and Midnight as first-class citizens, with addresses derived for the chains you also live on.

  • Cardano, fully. Balances, native assets and NFTs, multi-recipient sends, staking and DRep delegation, swaps, shared multi-signature wallets.
  • Midnight, including the shielded side. Unshielded, shielded and DUST addresses, side by side.
  • Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana addresses derived from the same recovery phrase, so you can prove on-chain ownership of them. Balances and sending for those three are not here yet, and the app says so rather than pretending.
  • Hardware and air-gapped. Ledger and Trezor for the non-Midnight chains, plus QR signing for a device that never touches a network.
Transaction review

Read the transaction, not a summary of it

Before anything is signed, Pnyx shows you what it will actually do — grouped by whose money is moving, down to every input, every output, every token in the bag, and the raw bytes if you want them. The wallet knows which addresses are yours, so "sending to yourself" looks like what it is instead of looking like a loss.

The same review surface is used everywhere a signature is requested — a send you started, a dApp that asked, or a co-signer's proposal. One screen to learn, not five.

Security

The boring parts, taken seriously

Self-custody is only a gift if losing your phone is survivable. These are the parts we would not ship without.

No app password, ever

Your recovery phrase lives in the phone's hardware keystore and is released by biometrics or your device passcode. There is no app-level password to guess, phish, or forget.

Passkey recovery

Restore an account on a new device with your face or fingerprint, using platform passkeys and the WebAuthn PRF extension.

Encrypted export you can keep

An open, documented backup format (CIP-0301) sealed with Argon2id and XChaCha20-Poly1305. Your password, your file, our maths — all of it inspectable.

Hardware wallets

Ledger and Trezor are supported for the non-Midnight chains, so the key can stay somewhere the phone can never reach it.

Air-gapped signing

Move a transaction to an offline device and its signature back, entirely by QR code.

A signing history

Every signature the wallet has produced, listed. Nothing is signed quietly in the background.

Shared wallets

Multi-signature wallets with co-signers, from creation through to collecting the last signature.

dApp connections you control

CIP-30 connections on mobile, each one listed and revocable from a single screen.

Twelve languages

English plus eleven more, with number and currency formatting that respects where you actually live.

Under the hood

For the people who will ask

One codebase, React Native on both platforms, Effect-TS end to end. The zero-knowledge prover is native Rust running on the handset — not a proving service we could quietly read. We run our own Midnight indexer rather than renting somebody else's view of the chain.

The contracts are written in Compact. Transaction fees for community actions are sponsored, so joining a conversation does not require first acquiring a token.

You can read all of it: the app, the contracts, the chain and crypto layers are open source under Apache-2.0. A wallet asking to hold your keys should be auditable by the people it asks.

Get the app

A pre-release build, handed to you directly

Pnyx is not on Google Play or the App Store yet. You install it yourself, from the file below. That means no automatic updates, a few extra taps, and some warnings from your phone that are worth reading rather than clicking past.

Read this before you install

  • This is not production software. It is an early build on a test network, published to be broken. Expect bugs, expect screens to change, and expect to lose what you put in it.
  • Do not put real funds in a wallet this app generates. The keys it creates live on a phone running pre-release code. If you want to touch real assets, connect a hardware wallet (Ledger or Trezor) so the key stays on the device and never enters the app.
  • Communities, posts and votes are disposable. Nothing on the test network carries over to mainnet.
  • Nobody from Pnyx will ever ask for your recovery phrase. Not in a community, not in a DM, not by email. Anyone who does is stealing from you.

iPhone

Not yet

iOS cannot be installed from a plain downloaded file the way Android can. Apple will only run an app that is signed — either for a named list of devices, or through TestFlight. Both need an Apple Developer Program membership, and that is the piece Pnyx does not have yet.

  1. The iOS app is built and working, on-device zero-knowledge proving included. The build is not what is missing.
  2. Signing a build with a free Apple ID is not a way round it: those profiles die after seven days, and they cannot carry the entitlements Pnyx needs for passkey recovery and deep links. A build with those stripped would look like Pnyx with its recovery broken.
  3. When it opens, the invite will be a TestFlight link or a signed install link from this domain. Treat any loose “.ipa download” claiming to be Pnyx as fake.

In the meantime, if you have a Mac, the Simulator build below runs the real iOS app.

Where things stand

Early, honest, and on a test network

Pnyx is in early access and currently runs against Midnight's test network. Treat everything in it as disposable: communities, posts and votes will not carry over when it moves to mainnet. The point right now is to find what breaks.
